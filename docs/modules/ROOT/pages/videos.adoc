== Testing your API timeouts with latency

A slow dependency is sneakier than a failing one: it answers, just too late. This video injects latency layer by layer and watches what the timeouts really do: a timeout that never sees the latency, a timeout doing its job, the grey zone around the timeout, a service without a timeout, retries that multiply the waiting time, and an average response time that hides the tail. About six minutes.

The key takeaways:

. Put a timeout on every remote call, and check that it covers the slow part: a fault injected on the inbound HTTP boundary hits the request before the guarded method runs (see xref:how-it-works.adoc#chaos-layers[Chaos layers]).
. Set it above your normal p99, or it cuts healthy requests.
. Count the real budget: timeout × (retries + 1) + delays, and keep it below the timeout of your own caller.
. Add a fallback and a circuit breaker to fail fast when the dependency stops answering.
. Watch percentiles, not the average.

To replay these scenarios on your own application, see xref:end-to-end-example.adoc[].
